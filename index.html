Crawlspace

How to Play

Tap a topic to open it.

The goal

Fight your way down five floors. Every floor is a new random maze. The stairs down sit at the far end of each floor, and an enemy always guards them. On floor 5 the stairs are replaced by the exit, daylight spilling through it, and the Ogre King stands before it. Beat him and you win: you're free.

You have one life. If your health hits 0, the run ends and you see a summary of how far you got.

The title screen keeps count: a skull for every run that ended in death and a crown for every escape, oldest first.

Moving and the map

Tap the side of the map you want to step toward: top, bottom, left or right of the middle. Small gold arrows at the edges of the map show which ways are open. Your torch lights the tiles right around you, and the path you have already walked stays on the map, dim. Everything else stays dark, including chests, shops and enemies, so explore to find them. Each floor has its own look, and its name shows under the map.

The bar along the top of the screen shows your level and floor, your health (the number sits on the bar) and the thin gold XP bar under it, your gold next to the coin, and your potions next to the flask. On a phone the game fills the screen; hold it upright. On a computer it plays in a phone-shaped column in the middle of the window. A bigger phone shows the same map, just larger.

Your buttons sit at the bottom of the screen, within reach of your thumb. They change with the moment: Fight and Flee when you meet enemies, Normal, Heavy and Block in a fight, Equip and Leave when you find gear. Hero and Bag are always there. A list too long for the screen, like a merchant's wares on a short screen, scrolls: drag it, or turn the mouse wheel over it.

The line just under the map is the message log. It shows what just happened, one message at a time (the dots on its right show how many there were). Tap it to read the whole history; Older and Newer page through it.

  • You: a small figure with a torch, wearing your gear
  • Chest: coins, a potion or a piece of gear. Its lid stands open once looted.
  • Capsa, a leather scroll case standing on end: one on every floor, always with a scroll in it. Its lid lies beside it once opened.
  • Stairs down, glowing faintly blue. Step on them to go to the next floor.
  • Merchant, hooded, at a market stall under a striped awning: floor 2 (Iron gear) and floor 4 (Steel gear)
  • Campfire, a ring of stones round a fire: floor 3, and the last camp halfway through floor 5
  • The exit, a doorway full of daylight: floor 5

On floors 2 and 4 the Merchant stands about halfway to the stairs, at a spot you can't get around, and on floors 3 and 5 a campfire does the same (on floor 5, on the way to the Ogre King). Everything in front of it is the easier half of the floor; the harder enemies, and the stairs, all lie beyond it, so you always reach it before them.

The campfire lasts for one thing before it burns out. Rest to recover 40% of your max HP, or Train for XP (80 on floor 3, 150 on floor 5: often enough for a level-up, with its perk). Leave it and it keeps burning, so you can clear the far half first and come back to it before you take the stairs, or face the King.

An enemy you have met stays on the map, standing on a ring in its color, with a number if it is a group. A white ring marks the Ogre King.

Fighting

Walk into an enemy to start a fight. The view switches to the combat screen: you stand on the left, facing the enemies on the right, each drawn as its own creature with its name and a green health bar over its head. Every floor has its own room, and the Ogre King fights you in his own hall. Enemies can come in groups of up to 3 on deeper floors; a group stands spread out in depth, the ones further back smaller. A * after a name means that enemy carries gear, so it hits harder or has armor.

Enemies wear the gear they carry, just like you. A sword in hand means it hits harder; a helmet, body armor, bracers or greaves mean it takes less damage. An enemy with a shield sometimes catches your hit on it and takes only half (a wooden one 15% of the time, iron 20%, steel 25%). Leather is brown, iron is grey and steel shines silver. Only Goblins, Skeletons, Orcs and Dark Knights carry gear. Their gear is never better than the floor allows: leather on floors 1 and 2, up to iron on floor 3, and steel only from floor 4. Every other enemy fights with teeth, claws and fists.

Tap an enemy to target it. A yellow ring under an enemy shows your target, and your attacks go to it. When your target falls, the next enemy is targeted for you.

Damage rises over the one who took it as a red number, healing as a green one. MISS appears over whoever an attack was aimed at, STUNNED over a stunned enemy, and SECOND WIND over you when that perk saves your life.

You first choose Fight or Flee. Fleeing works 25% of the time (up to 35% with Fleetfoot greaves) and puts you back one step. If it fails, the enemies get a free attack. You can never flee the Ogre King.

Each turn of a fight, pick one move. The enemies then take their turn.

  • Normal: your reliable hit, 8-14 damage plus your weapon and bonuses, minus the enemy's armor. It misses 10% of the time, and 10% of hits are critical hits for double damage.
  • Heavy: 1.75 times the damage, but it misses 45% of the time, and enemies hit you 20% harder that turn. Use it to finish a fight faster when you can afford the risk.
  • Block: you don't attack, but you take less damage. Without a shield Block stops 45% of each hit; a shield raises that (Wooden Buckler 55%, Iron Shield 65%, Steel Kite Shield 75%, the most it can be). Each hit you block has a 10% chance to stun that enemy so it skips its next attack. A shield raises the stun chance. Stuns don't chain: an enemy that was just stunned can't be stunned by its very next attack (another enemy in the group still can). Thorns doesn't cut enemies on a turn you Block.

Enemies miss 10% of the time too, and 10% of their hits are critical hits for 1.5 times the damage. Your armor takes points off every hit, but a hit always does at least 1.

When a fight ends you get coins and XP for each enemy, and the group drops a piece of gear. A card shows what you found (Found, on the left) next to what you have in that slot (Equipped, on the right): each piece's symbol (its shape and color show the material: brown leather, grey iron, bright steel trimmed in gold), its number (damage for a weapon, defense for armor, block for a shield), and its enchantment as a symbol and a few words. Between them a badge tells you at a glance: a green arrow up for Better, a red arrow down for Worse, = for Same, or a yellow Trade-off when it is better one way and worse another (say, a bigger number but no enchantment). You can Equip it or Leave it, and either one takes you back to the map. The two buttons fade in for a moment before they work, so a tap meant for the fight can't grab the item, and both take two taps: tap Equip (or Leave), then tap it again to confirm.

Enemies and their tricks

Every enemy has a special ability. The message log tells you when it happens.

Floors 1-2

  • Bat: hard to hit. You miss it 30% of the time instead of 10%, so Heavy is a poor choice.
  • Slime: when killed it splits into two Little Slimes (weak, but they still hit you).
  • Goblin: each hit has a 33% chance to steal 3-6 of your coins (once). Three turns later it runs off with them. Kill it first to get your coins back.
  • Skeleton (floor 2 on): the first time it dies it may pull itself back together (33%) with a quarter of its health, unless the killing blow was a critical hit.

Floors 2-4

  • Orc (deep in floor 2 on): bloodlust. Hits 4 harder while you are below half health, so heal before it gets going.
  • Troll (floor 3 on): regenerates 6 health every turn. A critical hit stops it for one turn, and fire stops it while the Troll burns. Finish Trolls fast.

Floors 3-5

  • Wraith: heals itself for a quarter of the damage it deals to you.

Floors 4-5

  • Dark Knight: often well armored. Whenever you miss it, it counterattacks for free, so a Heavy miss is costly.
  • Wyvern: each hit has a 35% chance to poison you: 3 damage every turn and every step for 4 turns. A potion cures poison. Your health bar turns purple while you are poisoned.

Floor 5

  • Minotaur: its first attack in a fight is a charge that never misses and deals double damage. Block it.

The Ogre King

475 health and armor of his own, and he hits for 22-29. Every third attack is a Crushing Blow that ignores your armor. The turn before, the log warns you: "The Ogre King winds up a huge swing..." That is your cue to Block or drink a potion. Below half health he flies into a rage and hits 3 harder.

Health and potions

You start with 100 health and one potion, and health does not come back on its own. Your ways to heal:

  • Potions: heal 40 (more with Antidote or Fortitude gear) and cure poison. Tap the potion flask in the top bar while exploring or during a fight (drinking in a fight uses your turn). The flask glows green when you're hurt enough to use a whole potion, and purple while you're poisoned.
  • Level-ups: +10 max HP (already filled in), then heal 16% of your max HP.
  • Some perks and enchantments, like Vitality, Mending and Vampiric.

You can carry 5 potions. The Potion Belt (40 coins, every merchant) raises that to 7, and the Potion Bandolier (70 coins, floor 4, needs the Belt) to 10. Each merchant has 5 potions for sale at 15 coins. If a chest holds a potion while your bag is full, you get coins instead.

Levels and perk cards

Kills give XP (bigger enemies give more), shown by the thin gold bar under your health. The first level needs 25 XP, and each level after needs 20 more than the last.

Each level-up gives you +10 max HP (already filled in), heals 16% of your max HP and deals you three perk cards. Pick one. A card shows the total you would have after taking it, and its edge shows how rare it is: grey is common, blue uncommon, gold rare. One card is always a basic. Don't like the hand? Redraw costs 10 coins, then 20, then 30 in the same level-up.

Every perk except Pathfinder, Second Wind and Tight Purse can be taken again and again. The specials grow a little less with each repeat, and specials you already own turn up more often. Pathfinder, Second Wind and Tight Purse can only be taken once, and once you have one it is never dealt again.

Basics

  • Attack: more damage on every hit. Your first pick gives +2, and the total grows to +3, +4, +5, +6 with more picks.
  • Vitality: +25 max health (and heals 25), and you heal 1 after every fight you win.
  • Focus: +4% critical hit chance, and every miss chance shrinks by 10% (including Heavy and against Bats).

Specials

  • Pathfinder (rare, once): see 1 more tile around you, and enemies within sight show on the map, with their gear and group size, before you meet them.
  • Ambusher: your first hit in every fight is an Ambush for +35% damage (a miss doesn't use it up). More picks raise it to +47%, +52% and so on. Running away and coming back doesn't give you a second ambush on the same group.
  • Executioner: +50% damage to enemies below half health.
  • Antidote: potions heal 10 more, and poison is weaker and shorter.
  • Shield Bash: when you Block, you also bash your target for 5 damage, and the stun chance rises to 21%.
  • Second Wind (rare, once): the first blow that would kill you leaves you at 10% of your max health instead. It saves you one time.
  • Tight Purse: Goblins can't steal from you, you find 30% more gold, and you deal +1 damage for every 80 coins you carry (up to +2). Spending your gold lowers the damage bonus, so you choose between buying now and hitting harder.
Gear and armor

You have six slots: weapon, shield, helmet, chest, bracers and greaves. Gear comes in three materials:

  • Basic: Rusty Sword, Wooden Buckler, Leather Cap, Leather Vest, Cloth Wraps, Leather Boots
  • Iron: Iron Sword, Iron Shield, Iron Helm, Chainmail, Iron Bracers, Iron Greaves
  • Steel: Steel Sword, Steel Kite Shield, Steel Helm, Steel Plate, Steel Bracers, Steel Greaves

A weapon adds its number to your damage. Armor numbers add up to your Defense, which comes off every enemy hit. Full Steel armor gives 13. What you find depends on the floor: floor 1 is mostly leather with the odd iron piece (but no Chainmail yet), floor 2 is half leather and half iron, Steel first turns up on floor 3, and floor 5 holds only iron and steel. A shield adds nothing to Defense; it makes Block stronger instead: each point stops 10% more of a blocked hit and adds 4% to the stun chance (with no shield Block stops 45%; a Steel Kite Shield, +3, takes it to 75%, the most it can be, and the stun chance from 10% to 22%). Gear drops from fights and chests, and merchants sell it (price = 5 coins per point). Better material also lets you use stronger enchantments.

Everything you wear shows on your character in fights, from a rusty sword to a steel great helm. You start out in a prisoner's rags: a torn tunic, a bandaged arm and bare feet. Wear all four armor pieces (helmet, chest, bracers and greaves) with a steel helm, and a knight's blue plume streams from it. A full set of Steel adds gold trim, and enchanted gear glows in its enchantment's color. Enemies show their gear the same way (see Fighting).

Tap Hero to see yourself in everything you wear, with your six slots around you. Each slot shows its piece's symbol, shaped and colored by its material (a rusty sword, an iron sword, a gold-guarded steel sword, and so on), and its number. An enchanted piece glows and shows its enchantment's symbol, with one, two or three dots for Minor, Good or Major. Below are your stats, each with its symbol (the damage range of your hits, Defense, Max HP, how much Block stops, stun, critical hit, miss and flee chances) and your perks. Tap any piece of gear or perk to read what it does. The Bag tab holds your scrolls.

Enchantments and scrolls

Any piece of gear can hold one enchantment. It stays on that item, so if you swap the item for another, its enchantment goes with it. The same enchantment on several items stacks.

Enchantments come in three tiers. Minor fits any gear, Good needs Iron or Steel gear, and Major needs Steel gear. Numbers below are Minor / Good / Major.

Weapon

  • Flaming: hits set the enemy on fire for 3 / 4 / 5 damage a turn for 3 turns. A burning Troll can't regenerate.
  • Vampiric: heal 4% / 6% / 9% of the damage you deal.
  • Balanced: Heavy misses 25% / 35% / 45% less often.
  • Giantslayer: +20% / 40% / 55% damage to big enemies (100+ health).
  • Swift: 12% / 20% / 26% chance to strike a second time.

Armor and shield

  • Thorns: enemies that hit you take 2 / 3 / 4 damage, but not on a turn you Block.
  • Mending: heal 4 / 7 / 10 after every fight you win.
  • Fortitude: +20 / 35 / 50 max health, and each potion heals 1 / 2 / 3 more.
  • Purity: poison lasts 1 / 2 / 3 fewer turns, and Wraith drain is cut 25% / 50% / 75%.
  • Fleetfoot (greaves only): +6% / 8% / 10% chance to flee.
  • Fortune: +10% / 20% / 30% gold.

Where to get them

  • Enchanted drops: 1 in 5 pieces of gear comes enchanted. Good ones show up from floor 2, Major from floor 4.
  • Scrolls: found in capsae, the leather scroll cases (one on every floor; chests never hold scrolls). Put it on a piece of gear right away, keep it, or throw it away. Your bag holds 2 scrolls (3 with the Scroll Satchel, 25 coins at the merchants). Tap Bag and then Use to put a kept one on later (while exploring), for example once you find Iron gear for a Good scroll.

You can't pick your enchantments: build around what the dungeon gives you. A scroll you don't want sells at any merchant. Putting a new enchantment on an item replaces the old one. Putting a scroll on gear, and throwing one away, take two taps: the first shows what will happen, the second does it.

Gold and shops

Gold comes from kills and chests, and there is never enough of it. Spend it at the merchants on floors 2 and 4 (potions, potion bags, the Scroll Satchel and gear) and on Redraws. The floor 4 merchant is the last shop: there is nothing to buy on floor 5. Whatever you still carry when you escape goes to the Vault.

You can also sell. Tap Sell at a merchant to sell potions, any piece of gear you wear, or scrolls from your bag. You get 25% of what the thing is worth: gear is worth 5 coins a point, and an enchantment is worth 15 (Minor), 30 (Good) or 50 (Major), on gear or as a scroll. Buying and selling take two taps: tap a row, then tap it again (it turns gold while it waits), so you never buy or sell by accident. Right after you buy something, that row stays ready for a few seconds, so a second potion is one tap. Gear you sell leaves its slot empty.

Buying gear for a slot you already fill trades in your old piece: the merchant buys it for the same 25%, and that counts toward the price. The shop list shows what your piece is worth as "trade-in". If your old piece is enchanted, the enchantment goes with it.

Tips
  • Clear each floor before taking the stairs. Every fight is XP, gold and a chance at gear.
  • Drink potions between fights, not halfway through a dangerous one, unless the next hit could kill you.
  • Block the Ogre King's Crushing Blow and the Minotaur's opening charge.
  • Kill Trolls fast, and kill Goblins before they run off with your gold.
  • Skip Heavy against Bats and Dark Knights, where misses hurt most.
  • Save a potion or two for the Ogre King.
  • The top bar shows your level, floor, health, XP, gold and potions. Tap Hero for your gear, stats and perks, and Bag for your scrolls.
The Vault

Escape, and every coin you still carry goes into the Vault (tap Vault on the title screen). Die, and your coins are lost with you. So at the last merchant there is a choice: spend to get stronger, or save for the Vault.

The Vault sells looks: the color of your torch's flame (Ghostfire, Witchfire or Soulfire), your hair, a beard, the dye of your prisoner's rags, and the plume you wear in full armor with a Steel helm. They change nothing but how you look. Tap a look to try it on, tap it again to buy it, and tap one you own to wear it.

The Vault

Escape the dungeon, and the coins you carry out are kept here. Spend them on how you look; it changes nothing else.
