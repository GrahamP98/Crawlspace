Crawlspace

How to Play

Tap a topic to open it.

The goal

Fight your way down five floors. Every floor is a new random maze. The stairs down sit at the far end of each floor, and an enemy always guards them. On floor 5 the stairs are replaced by the exit, daylight spilling through it, and the Ogre King stands before it. Beat him and you win: you're free.

You have one life. If your health hits 0, the run ends and you see a summary of how far you got.

Your run is saved as you play. Close the game, take a call or go back to the title, and Continue on the title screen picks it up exactly where you left off, with the floor and level you were on written under it. It is saved after every move, so reopening the game never undoes a bad turn. New run starts over and throws the saved run away, so it takes two taps. A run that ends, in death or escape, leaves nothing to continue.

The title screen keeps count: a skull for every run that ended in death and a crown for every escape, oldest first.

Moving and the map

Tap the side of the map you want to step toward: top, bottom, left or right of the middle. Small gold arrows at the edges of the map show which ways are open. Your torch lights the tiles right around you, and the path you have already walked stays on the map, dim. Everything else stays dark, including chests, shops and enemies, so explore to find them. Each floor has its own look, and its name shows under the map.

The bar along the top of the screen shows your level and floor, your health (the number sits on the bar) and the thin gold XP bar under it, your gold next to the coin, and your potions next to the flask. On a phone the game fills the screen; hold it upright. On a computer it plays in a phone-shaped column in the middle of the window. A bigger phone shows the same map, just larger.

Your buttons sit at the bottom of the screen, within reach of your thumb. They change with the moment: Fight and Flee when you meet enemies, Normal, Heavy and Block in a fight, Equip and Leave when you find gear. Hero and Bag are always there. A list too long for the screen, like a merchant's wares on a short screen, scrolls: drag it, or turn the mouse wheel over it.

The line just under the map is the message log. It shows what just happened, one message at a time (the dots on its right show how many there were). Tap it to read the whole history; Older and Newer page through it.

  • You: a small figure with a torch, wearing your gear
  • Chest: coins, a potion or a piece of gear. Its lid stands open once looted.
  • Capsa, a leather scroll case standing on end: one on every floor, always with a scroll in it. Its lid lies beside it once opened.
  • Stairs down, glowing faintly blue. Step on them to go to the next floor.
  • Merchant, hooded, in a covered wagon with its side rolled up as a shopfront: floor 2 (Iron gear) and floor 4 (Steel gear). Once you leave, he rolls on and only his wheel ruts are left
  • Campfire, a ring of stones round a fire: floor 3, and the last camp halfway through floor 5
  • The exit, a doorway full of daylight: floor 5

On floors 2 and 4 the Merchant stands about halfway to the stairs, at a spot you can't get around, and on floors 3 and 5 a campfire does the same (on floor 5, on the way to the Ogre King). Everything in front of it is the easier half of the floor; the harder enemies, and the stairs, all lie beyond it, so you always reach it before them.

The campfire lasts for one thing before it burns out. Rest to recover 60% of your max HP, or Train for XP (110 on floor 3, 200 on floor 5: often enough for a level-up, with its perk). Choose before you walk on: once you leave it, the fire goes out and you can't come back to it.

An enemy you have met stays on the map, standing on a ring in its color, with a number if it is a group. A white ring marks the Ogre King.

Fighting

Walk into an enemy to start a fight. The view switches to the combat screen: you stand on the left, facing the enemies on the right, each drawn as its own creature with its name and a green health bar over its head. Every floor has its own room, and the Ogre King fights you in his own hall. Enemies can come in pairs, and from floor 3 in groups of 3 (the enemy guarding the stairs never brings more than one other); a group stands spread out in depth, the ones further back smaller. A * after a name means that enemy carries gear, so it hits harder or has armor. A red V after a name, and a red glow round it, mark a veteran (see below).

Enemies wear the gear they carry, just like you. A sword in hand means it hits harder; a helmet, body armor, bracers or greaves mean it takes less damage. An enemy with a shield sometimes catches your hit on it and takes only half (a wooden one 15% of the time, iron 20%, steel 25%). Leather is brown, iron is grey and steel shines silver. Only Goblins, Skeletons, Orcs and Dark Knights carry gear. Their gear is never better than the floor allows: leather on floors 1 and 2, up to iron on floor 3, and steel only from floor 4. Every other enemy fights with teeth, claws and fists.

Tap an enemy to target it. A yellow ring under an enemy shows your target, and your attacks go to it. When your target falls, the next enemy is targeted for you.

Damage rises over the one who took it as a red number, healing as a green one. MISS appears over whoever an attack was aimed at, STUNNED over a stunned enemy, and SECOND WIND over you when that perk saves your life.

You first choose Fight or Flee. Fleeing works 25% of the time (up to 35% with Fleetfoot greaves) and puts you back one step. If it fails, the enemies get a free attack. You can never flee the Ogre King.

Every enemy shows what it will do next: a small badge over its name. A sword is an attack, and the number beside it is about how much it will hurt you (before your Block or Heavy). A shield, a bat or an arrow is a stance: it is up during your turn (hard to hit, braced, guarding, out of reach), and the enemy doesn't attack on its turn. A heart is a heal, a horn a war cry, and so on (see Enemies and their tricks). A red badge is a big move: it always lands, so Block it. A move you see always happens, unless you stun the enemy first: blue stars mean it will skip its turn. In a group, only one enemy at a time shows a big move, and only one a stance.

A big move leaves the enemy Off balance (it says so after its name) until it acts again: your hits on it can't miss, its shield can't catch them and it can't strike back. That is the moment for a Heavy.

Each turn of a fight, pick one move. The enemies then take their turn. If every enemy is out of reach, Normal and Heavy raise your guard instead.

  • Normal: your reliable hit, 8-14 damage plus your weapon and bonuses, less the share the enemy's armor cuts. It misses 10% of the time, and 10% of hits are critical hits for double damage.
  • Heavy: 1.75 times the damage (more with a Balanced weapon), but it misses 45% of the time, and enemies hit you 20% harder that turn. Use it to finish a fight faster when you can afford the risk.
  • Block: you don't attack, but you take less damage. Without a shield Block stops 45% of each hit; a shield raises that (Wooden Buckler 55%, Iron Shield 65%, Steel Kite Shield 70%, the most it can be). Each hit you block has a 10% chance to stun that enemy so it skips its next attack. A shield raises the stun chance. Stuns don't chain: an enemy that was just stunned can't be stunned by its very next attack (another enemy in the group still can). Thorns doesn't cut enemies on a turn you Block.

Enemies miss 10% of the time too, and 10% of their hits are critical hits for 1.5 times the damage. Your armor cuts a share off every hit (3.5% for each point of Defense, 45% at most), and enemy armor cuts your hits the same way. A hit always does at least 1.

When a fight ends you get coins and XP for each enemy, and the group drops a piece of gear. A card shows what you found (Found, on the left) next to what you have in that slot (Equipped, on the right): each piece's symbol (its shape and color show the material: brown leather, grey iron, bright steel trimmed in gold), its number (damage for a weapon, defense for armor, block for a shield), and its enchantment as a symbol and a few words. Between them a badge tells you at a glance: a green arrow up for Better, a red arrow down for Worse, = for Same, or a yellow Trade-off when it is better one way and worse another (say, a bigger number but no enchantment). You can Equip it or Leave it, and either one takes you back to the map. The two buttons fade in for a moment before they work, so a tap meant for the fight can't grab the item, and both take two taps: tap Equip (or Leave), then tap it again to confirm.

Enemies and their tricks

Every enemy has a special ability, and most have a move or two besides their attack. Their moves follow a pattern, so you can learn them; the badge over each enemy shows the next one. Some moves need gear: only an enemy with a shield can Brace, Guard or Cower, and only a Dark Knight with a steel sword can Sunder. The message log tells you when anything happens.

Each floor has its own kinds in its first half and tougher ones past the merchant or campfire. Some groups in the far half are led by a veteran of the first half's kinds: the same enemy, with more health and a harder hit. A veteran has a red V after its name and glows red in the fight. The enemy guarding the stairs is always one of the floor's tougher kind.

Sundered: acid and a Sundering blow take 1 off your Defense each (2 at most, so your armor cuts up to 7% less) until the fight ends. Shaken: after a Wail your next attack does 30% less. Both show under your health in a fight.

Floors 1-2

  • Bat: hard to hit. You miss it 30% of the time instead of 10%, so Heavy is a poor choice. Every third turn it Flits (bat): it darts about in the dark and you miss it 70% of the time. Hit something else, or Block.
  • Slime: when killed it splits into two Little Slimes (weak, but they still hit you). Every third turn it spits acid (drop), which Sunders your armor.
  • Goblin: every other turn it tries to Snatch (hand) 3-6 of your coins (once). Block and it gets nothing. If it gets them, three turns later it runs off (arrow). Kill it first to get your coins back. A Goblin with a shield, in a group, sometimes Cowers behind the others, out of reach for that turn.
  • Skeleton (floor 2 on): the first time it dies it may pull itself back together (33%) with a quarter of its health, unless the killing blow was a critical hit. With a shield, every third turn it Braces (shield): your hits do half, so don't waste a Heavy on it.

Floors 2-4

  • Orc (deep in floor 2 on): bloodlust. Hits 4 harder while you are below half health, so heal before it gets going. In a group its first move is a War cry (horn): the rest of the group hits 2 harder while the Orc lives. Kill it first. Only one Orc in a group cries: the others just attack, so war cries never stack.
  • Troll (floor 3 on): regenerates 6 health every turn. A critical hit stops it for one turn, and fire stops it while the Troll burns. Finish Trolls fast. Every third turn it hurls a Boulder (a big move: Block it). Once, below half health, it Licks its wounds (heart) for 18 and doesn't attack: a free Heavy for you (fire stops the healing).

Floors 3-5

  • Wraith: heals itself for a quarter of the damage it deals to you. Its badge shows only a ?: you never know if it will strike or Wail (skull), which Shakes you. Pathfinder sees through it.

Floors 4-5

  • Dark Knight: often well armored. Whenever you miss it, it counterattacks for free, so a Heavy miss is costly. With a shield, every third turn it holds its Guard (shield with a red cross): it strikes back at every attack, hit or miss, so Block, drink, or hit another enemy. With a steel sword it brings down a Sundering blow (a big move) that dents your armor.
  • Wyvern: each hit has a 35% chance to poison you: 3 damage every turn and every step for 4 turns. A potion cures poison. Your health bar turns purple while you are poisoned. After two bites it Takes wing (up arrow), out of reach, then Dives (down arrow) for double damage. Block the dive, then Heavy it while it is Off balance.

Floor 5

  • Minotaur: its first move in a fight is a Charge (horns on red) that never misses and deals double damage. Block it. Every fourth turn it paws the ground (horns) and charges again the turn after.

The Ogre King

475 health and armor of his own, and he hits for 14-18. Every third attack is a Crushing Blow (hammer) that ignores your armor. The turn before, his badge turns red and the log warns you: "The Ogre King winds up a huge swing..." That is your cue to Block or drink a potion. Below half health he flies into a rage and hits 3 harder. At two thirds of his health he Roars (horn) for a Goblin servant, and once below half he Feasts (heart), healing 40 without attacking. When he falls, his servant flees.

Health and potions

You start with 100 health and one potion, and health does not come back on its own. Your ways to heal:

  • Potions: heal 40 (more with Antidote or Fortitude gear) and cure poison. Tap the potion flask in the top bar while exploring or during a fight (drinking in a fight uses your turn). The flask glows green when you're hurt enough to use a whole potion, and purple while you're poisoned.
  • Level-ups: +10 max HP (already filled in), then heal 16% of your max HP.
  • Some perks and enchantments, like Vitality, Mending and Vampiric.

You can carry 5 potions. The Potion Belt (40 coins, every merchant) raises that to 7, and the Potion Bandolier (70 coins, floor 4, needs the Belt) to 10. Each merchant has 5 potions for sale at 15 coins. If a chest holds a potion while your bag is full, you get coins instead.

Levels and perk cards

Kills give XP (bigger enemies give more), shown by the thin gold bar under your health. The first level needs 25 XP, and each level after needs 20 more than the last.

Each level-up gives you +10 max HP (already filled in), heals 16% of your max HP and deals you three perk cards. Pick one. The cards are dealt like tarot cards. Each shows its rank (a numeral: how many times you will have it), the total you would have after taking it, and its frame shows how rare it is: plain iron is common, silver with studs uncommon, gilt with gold corners rare. One card is always a basic. Don't like the hand? Redraw costs 10 coins, then 20, then 30 in the same level-up.

Every perk except Pathfinder, Second Wind and Tight Purse can be taken again and again. The specials grow a little less with each repeat, and specials you already own turn up more often. Pathfinder, Second Wind and Tight Purse can only be taken once, and once you have one it is never dealt again.

Basics

  • Attack: more damage on every hit. Your first pick gives +2, and the total grows to +3, +4, +5, +6 with more picks.
  • Vitality: +20 max health (and heals 20), and you heal 1 after every fight you win.
  • Focus: +4% critical hit chance, and every miss chance shrinks by 10% (including Heavy and against Bats).

Specials

  • Pathfinder (rare, once): see 1 more tile around you, and enemies within sight show on the map, with their gear and group size, before you meet them. In a fight you also see each enemy's move after next (dimmed, beside its badge), and what a Wraith is about to do.
  • Ambusher: your first hit in every fight is an Ambush for +35% damage (a miss doesn't use it up). More picks raise it to +47%, +52% and so on. Running away and coming back doesn't give you a second ambush on the same group.
  • Executioner: +40% damage to enemies below half health. More picks raise it to +53%, +60% and so on.
  • Antidote: potions heal 8 more (10 with a second pick, 11 with a third), and poison is weaker and shorter.
  • Shield Bash: when you Block, you also bash your target for 3 damage, and the stun chance rises to 15%. More picks raise both (5 damage and 18% at two).
  • Second Wind (rare, once): the first blow that would kill you leaves you at 30% of your max health instead. It saves you one time.
  • Tight Purse: Goblins can't steal from you, you find 30% more gold, and you deal +1 damage for every 80 coins you carry (up to +2). Spending your gold lowers the damage bonus, so you choose between buying now and hitting harder.
Gear and armor

You have six slots: weapon, shield, helmet, chest, bracers and greaves. Gear comes in three materials:

  • Basic: Rusty Sword, Wooden Buckler, Leather Cap, Leather Vest, Cloth Wraps, Leather Boots
  • Iron: Iron Sword, Iron Shield, Iron Helm, Chainmail, Iron Bracers, Iron Greaves
  • Steel: Steel Sword, Steel Kite Shield, Steel Helm, Steel Plate, Steel Bracers, Steel Greaves

A weapon adds its number to your damage. Armor numbers add up to your Defense, and each point cuts 3.5% off every enemy hit, 45% at most: full Leather (5) cuts 18%, full Iron (9) 32%, and full Steel (13) the full 45%. The Hero tab shows how much yours cuts. What you find depends on the floor: floor 1 is mostly leather with the odd iron piece (but no Chainmail yet), floor 2 is half leather and half iron, Steel first turns up on floor 3, and floor 5 holds only iron and steel. A shield adds nothing to Defense; it makes Block stronger instead: each point stops 10% more of a blocked hit and adds 4% to the stun chance (with no shield Block stops 45%; a Steel Kite Shield, +3, takes it to 70%, the most it can be, and the stun chance from 10% to 22%). Gear drops from fights and chests, and merchants sell it (price = 5 coins per point). Better material also lets you use stronger enchantments.

Everything you wear shows on your character in fights, from a rusty sword to a steel great helm. You start out in a prisoner's rags: a torn tunic, a bandaged arm and bare feet. Wear all four armor pieces (helmet, chest, bracers and greaves) with a steel helm, and a knight's blue plume streams from it. A full set of Steel adds gold trim, and enchanted gear glows in its enchantment's color. Enemies show their gear the same way (see Fighting).

Tap Hero to see yourself in everything you wear, with your six slots around you. Each slot shows its piece's symbol, shaped and colored by its material (a rusty sword, an iron sword, a gold-guarded steel sword, and so on), and its number. An enchanted piece glows and shows its enchantment's symbol, with one, two or three dots for Minor, Good or Major. Below are your stats, each with its symbol (the damage range of your hits, Defense, Max HP, how much Block stops, stun, critical hit, miss and flee chances) and your perks. Tap any piece of gear or perk to read what it does. The Bag tab holds your scrolls.

Enchantments and scrolls

Any piece of gear can hold one enchantment. It stays on that item, so if you swap the item for another, its enchantment goes with it. The same enchantment on several items stacks.

Enchantments come in three tiers. Minor fits any gear, Good needs Iron or Steel gear, and Major needs Steel gear. Numbers below are Minor / Good / Major.

Weapon

  • Flaming: hits set the enemy on fire for 2 / 3 / 4 damage a turn for 3 turns. A burning Troll can't regenerate.
  • Vampiric: heal 4% / 6% / 9% of the damage you deal.
  • Balanced: Heavy misses 25% / 35% / 45% less often and hits 7% / 10% / 13% harder.
  • Giantslayer: +15% / 30% / 40% damage to big enemies: Orcs, Trolls, Minotaurs, Wyverns and the Ogre King.
  • Swift: 15% / 23% / 30% chance to strike a second time.

Armor and shield

  • Thorns: enemies that hit you take 1 / 2 / 3 damage, but not on a turn you Block.
  • Mending: heal 2 / 4 / 6 after every fight you win.
  • Fortitude: +20 / 30 / 40 max health, and each potion heals 1 / 2 / 3 more.
  • Purity: poison lasts 1 / 2 / 3 fewer turns, Wraith drain is cut 25% / 50% / 75%, and a 20% / 30% / 40% chance to shrug off armor loss from Slime acid or a Sundering blow.
  • Fleetfoot (greaves only): +6% / 8% / 10% chance to flee, and enemies miss you 5% / 8% / 10% more often (not their big moves).
  • Fortune: +10% / 20% / 30% gold.

Where to get them

  • Enchanted drops: 1 in 5 pieces of gear comes enchanted. Good ones show up from floor 2, Major from floor 4.
  • Scrolls: found in capsae, the leather scroll cases (one on every floor; chests never hold scrolls). Put it on a piece of gear right away, keep it, or throw it away. Your bag holds 2 scrolls (3 with the Scroll Satchel, 25 coins at the merchants). Tap Bag and then Use to put a kept one on later (while exploring), for example once you find Iron gear for a Good scroll.

You can't pick your enchantments: build around what the dungeon gives you. A scroll you don't want sells at any merchant. Putting a new enchantment on an item replaces the old one. Putting a scroll on gear, and throwing one away, take two taps: the first shows what will happen, the second does it.

Gold and shops

Gold comes from kills and chests, and there is never enough of it. A merchant trades with you only once: when you tap Leave (twice, to be sure) he rolls down his canvas and his wagon creaks away. Spend it at the merchants on floors 2 and 4 (potions, potion bags, the Scroll Satchel and gear) and on Redraws. The floor 4 merchant is the last shop: there is nothing to buy on floor 5. Whatever you still carry when you escape goes to the Vault.

You can also sell. Tap Sell at a merchant to sell potions, any piece of gear you wear, or scrolls from your bag. You get 25% of what the thing is worth: gear is worth 5 coins a point, and an enchantment is worth 15 (Minor), 30 (Good) or 50 (Major), on gear or as a scroll. Buying and selling take two taps: tap a row, then tap it again (it turns gold while it waits), so you never buy or sell by accident. Right after you buy something, that row stays ready for a few seconds, so a second potion is one tap. Gear you sell leaves its slot empty.

Buying gear for a slot you already fill trades in your old piece: the merchant buys it for the same 25%, and that counts toward the price. The shop list shows what your piece is worth as "trade-in". If your old piece is enchanted, the enchantment goes with it.

Tips
  • Clear each floor before taking the stairs. Every fight is XP, gold and a chance at gear.
  • Drink potions between fights, not halfway through a dangerous one, unless the next hit could kill you.
  • Block the Ogre King's Crushing Blow and the Minotaur's opening charge.
  • Kill Trolls fast, and kill Goblins before they run off with your gold.
  • Skip Heavy against Bats and Dark Knights, where misses hurt most.
  • Save a potion or two for the Ogre King.
  • The top bar shows your level, floor, health, XP, gold and potions. Tap Hero for your gear, stats and perks, and Bag for your scrolls.
The Vault

Escape, and every coin you still carry goes into the Vault (tap Vault on the title screen, or on the screen at the end of a run). Die, and your coins are lost with you. So at the last merchant there is a choice: spend to get stronger, or save for the Vault.

The Vault sells looks: the color of your torch's flame (Ghostfire, Witchfire or Soulfire), your hair, a beard, the dye of your prisoner's rags, and the plume you wear in full armor with a Steel helm. They change nothing but how you look. Tap a look to try it on, tap it again to buy it, and tap one you own to wear it.

Sound and music

Every floor has its own music, and so do fights, the Ogre King, the campfire and the merchant. A blow sounds like what it hits: a slime squelches, bones crack and rattle, flesh thuds, armor rings, and a wraith only sighs. When your health runs low in a fight, you hear your heart.

The gear opens the Settings: in the corner of the title screen, and in the top-right corner of the game. There you switch the Music and the Sounds on or off. In the game it also holds Return to the title. Your run is saved when you leave, and Continue brings you back to it.

The game keeps a play journal of your last 30 runs on your device: every fight, turn, perk, find and purchase. Save play journal in the game's Settings shares it (on a phone) or downloads it (on a computer) as a file, so it can be studied to balance the game. It never leaves your device unless you save it.
